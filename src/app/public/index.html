work

Some of it is cloud systems for companies. Some of it is getting a grandparent into their email. Either way, the goal is to leave people better off with their tech than they were before.

accounting firm · cloud & AI

Answers in seconds, with the source attached

Staff dug through Microsoft 365 for policies and client documents, or interrupted a senior colleague. I built an assistant on AWS Bedrock that answers only from approved documents the person is allowed to see, cites them, and says so when it doesn't know.

~5 min → 14 s median lookup · recall 72% → 94%

dermatology practice · workflows

Patient intake, out of the shared inbox

A new practice ran intake through one email inbox, chasing missing documents and retyping them into the patient record. I mapped the process with the staff and built a small portal on AWS. Patients upload once, and the office can see at a glance what's complete.

2.6 days → 3.4 hours turnaround · 24 → 8 min per intake

automotive business · migration

Two offices off local servers

The application, database, and files lived on two local servers. With their technical lead, I moved everything to AWS, linked both locations over a VPN, tested the real daily tasks from each office, and handed him an environment he runs himself.

2 servers, 2 locations · no app rewrite

schools, families, & teams · security

Security talks for people who aren't security people

Sessions for teachers, parents, engineers, and executives, including seminars in Manhattan for groups of 30 or more. Home and business networks, cameras and connected devices, fraud, data brokers, the dark web, and keeping physical spaces safe.

30+ people per seminar · free for the school that started it

family & friends · home tech

From a camera system to Grandma's email

I put a camera system in at my dad's house and moved family onto a password manager. Not every job is a cloud migration, and the small ones count. If someone you know just needs help opening their email, that's a fine reason to call.

small jobs welcome · patience included

about

neon1 started small, at a grade school. I'd wanted to put what I know to work in my own community for a long time, and that school was where I first got the chance. I helped budget and set up a new STEM edtech system, then invited teachers and parents to free sessions on staying safe online.

People left those sessions knowing a little more and worrying a little less. That's still the whole job: plain answers, a fixed price, accounts in your name, and docs you can actually use, so you're better off with your tech than before.

The name nods to Neo from The Matrix, who helps people see clearly, and to Andrew from Bicentennial Man, who spends two centuries being of service.

Neo means new. Neon is unmissable.

questions

Do you come on site?

Yes. I also work remotely, or on your own network through a secure connection that I set up for the job and that's removed when it's done.

When do you work?

Saturdays and Sundays from 8am to 8pm, and weekday evenings from 6pm (until 9pm Monday to Thursday, 8pm on Friday), Eastern time. That's about 10 to 20 hours a week of project time. The booking calendar shows what's open.

How long does a project take?

Small jobs take a week or two, medium ones two to four weeks, and bigger projects four to six. I take on up to two clients at a time so each one gets proper attention.

How is the price set?

After the free call I send a fixed price for the whole job. It doesn't change unless you change what you're asking for.

Do you run security training?

Yes, for anyone from parents and teachers to engineers and executives. I've run sessions in Manhattan for groups of 30 or more, covering home and business networks, cameras and IoT, fraud, data brokers, the dark web, and the security of physical spaces.

What don't you do?

I don't repair electronics, though I can debug software and hardware installs and get them working together. I don't handle personal or health data without the right agreements in place, such as a Business Associate Agreement, but I can help you get them. I'm not a lawyer, but I'll work alongside yours on the technical side.
