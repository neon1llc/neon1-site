how it works

Listen

Show me how the work gets done today. One real example is enough.

Propose

A few options, from the quickest fix to the fuller build, each with its price, effort, and what it gets you.

Decide

You pick. Sometimes that means a new system. Often it's a setting, a cleanup, or leaving it alone.

Build

Small changes, tested, and shown to you as they land.

Hand over

I show you how it works and write it down, so you can run it without me. Then I check it's doing what we agreed, and keep improving it if you want ongoing care.

about

neon1 grew out of something I'd wanted for a long time: to put what I know to work for the people and places around me. It started small, at a grade school, and the goal hasn't changed since. Leave people better off with their tech than they were before.

That comes down to a few habits. Listen before building anything. Keep it as simple as the problem allows. Make sure you own your accounts, your data, and the docs to run it. Be clear about what things cost and why. Plan for things to break, and check that the work actually does what it should.

The name nods to Neo from The Matrix, who brought peace between man and machine, and the slogan to Robin Williams' Andrew from Bicentennial Man, a machine who spends two centuries being of service while searching for his humanity.

Neo means new. Neon is unmissable.

questions

Do you come on site?

Yes. I also work remotely, or on your own network through a secure connection that I set up for the job and that's removed when it's done.

When do you work?

Saturdays and Sundays from 8am to 8pm, and weekday evenings from 6pm (until 9pm Monday to Thursday, 8pm on Friday), Eastern time. That's about 10 to 20 hours a week of project time. The booking calendar shows what's open.

How long does a project take?

Small jobs take a week or two, medium ones two to four weeks, and bigger projects four to six. I take on up to two clients at a time so each one gets proper attention.

How is the price set?

After the free call I send a short proposal with options, usually a quick fix, a solid setup, and a fuller build. Each one has a fixed price and says what it covers, how much effort and engineering it takes, and what quality to expect. You pick the one that fits, and that price holds unless you change what you're asking for.

Do you run security training?

Yes, for anyone from parents and teachers to engineers and executives. I've run sessions in Manhattan for groups of 30 or more, covering home and business networks, cameras and IoT, fraud, data brokers, the dark web, and the security of physical spaces.

What don't you do?

I don't repair electronics, though I can debug software and hardware installs and get them working together. I don't handle personal or health data without the right agreements in place, such as a Business Associate Agreement, but I can help you get them. I'm not a lawyer, but I'll work alongside yours on the technical side.
